> How to make kettle corn without burning the sugar?
> The key to making perfect homemade kettle corn is to constantly stir the kernels after mixing in sugar. This coats them evenly and prevents the ones in the bottom of the pot from sticking. Using the Popsmith Popper makes a huge difference. Its patented stirring mechanism keeps the kernels moving so nothing sits too long on the heat.

> Making this on an induction stove? The Popper's stainless steel, multi-clad bottom is fully induction compatible — just start on medium heat (level 4-6), keep the Popper centered on the burner, and be extra mindful of Step 4 timing since induction heats faster than gas or electric.

> Cleaning hardened sugar. Caramelized sugar can be particularly stubborn to remove. Give your Popper time to soak in warm water, and then gently scrub it with a non-abrasive brush before placing the pot inside the dishwasher.

> What gives kettle corn its flavor?
> Every kettle corn recipe requires sugar and salt to give it its distinctive flavor. This fusion of sweet and slightly savory makes it a popular treat at county fairs, although you really don't need an excuse to eat it any time of the year.
