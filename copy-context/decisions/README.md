# Decision library

Dave 2026-09-01: if it is not language, it is decision making. The talk library (`../transcripts/`) is how she talks. This library is which information earns the space.

Having the facts is not the job. Choosing which facts best serve the narrative and the agenda is the job. Hierarchy. Use of space. A reader who never owned the tired object still has to feel the problem.

Capture once. Annotate the page. Do not re-fetch it every week.


## Tests (Peggy, every H2 and every first line)

1. **Would a woman who never owned the competitor still feel the problem?** If she needs the object in her hand to know why this matters, it does not earn the space.
2. **Is this a part number or a night in the kitchen?** Two clips, nylon gears, aluminum as the headline: part numbers. Standing at the sink, hiding it, popcorn on the burner: the night.
3. **Does this fact serve the agenda?** The agenda is why she leaves The Popper out. Specs that do not move that story wait in the body, or they die.
4. **Use of space.** One idea. If you have five true things, pick the one that does the most narrative work. The rest can prove it underneath, or not appear.
5. **Does the next sentence follow?** Describe the scene so a stranger can see it. Stacked punchy lines that assume she already knows the object fail. The Tarly whole-paragraph saves are the cohesion bar. Named fail: `fails/2026-09-02-stacked-sentences.md`.
