# Tony Humrichouser, movie night with the pot

Weekends were always special in our house. Saturday and Sunday meant popcorn. Not microwave popcorn. Not bagged popcorn. Not anything you tore open and shook into a bowl. It meant my Dad’s popcorn.

Before we knew it, he was pouring popcorn into a bowl. It tasted like home. I did not know then that a person could taste safety.

Later that night, we made it. He read the directions. We followed them exactly. Every step. Every word. We watched the foil swell up like a little silver balloon. He cut it open. I took a bite. It was terrible. Greasy. Flat. Disappointing in a way that felt personal, as if the box had promised me something and then laughed. I was sad, because my Dad was setting us up to watch a movie, and movie nights mattered.

But before I could even say much, the pot was out. The oil was in. The kernels followed. Within ten minutes he repaired everything. The real popcorn returned, warm and forgiving. Then I climbed the stairs and sat in front of the TV with my Dad. Popcorn between us. Soda in hand. The world made right again.
