Then I wanted to finally try the antique Swedish hutch that I bought years ago for this house. It was a beast to move in here but we had movers doing other things on the property and I was like 'now's my chance!!'. So we shot it again to show you. And between the boring rug and the hutch something felt pretty off. I have found myself just staring at this room and wondering how to fix it since then.

But after being so incredibly frustrated with myself that I can't nail this room I've decided to do an experiment where I give myself permission to take the time to actually design it for the first time, with intention, instead of making last minute choices (the Real Simple shoot) or using product that I need to sell (my sofas and rugs). It's been really fun to fantasize about my dream floral sofa and shop for the perfect rug (while keeping most of the other elements that I love so much). I still want it to lean Scandinavian farmhouse, with color, but have it feel balanced and flow easily with the rest of the first floor and up the landing.

Meanwhile, I know for sure that I wanted a Persian-style vintage rug in the guest cottage, so I've been shopping and I actually found a one on Etsy with a good return policy that I'm trying out in here.

OK. Let's. Talk. About. It. I love that it has a lot of the tones that are already happening (the blue of the hutch, the warmer tones that work so well with the coffee table and the kitchen island). It's huge, 12x15, which is the size that this room wants and is so impossible to find vintage unless you spend like $15k, which I won't. When it arrived it was a bit muddier than I thought it would be and yet is still really pretty. I love that it's not super dark and busy, but it's not quite singing with everything else, either. But whose fault is that? The rug or the Barbs?

And if you are wondering if I plugged said vintage rug into AI to render it out first, I did and it looked pretty dang great. Good enough to help me order it to see it in person. But the colors ended up being off — it's deeper and darker in the render than in real life.

There are times when I love it and other times when I walk in and i'm like, 'Nope. Not there yet'. It's making me wonder if a 12x15 vintage rug exists that I will ever love (that is in a normal price range). But I wanted to shoot it and show you guys because that's what we do — try things, show and tell, and generally obsess about whether or not they are right.
