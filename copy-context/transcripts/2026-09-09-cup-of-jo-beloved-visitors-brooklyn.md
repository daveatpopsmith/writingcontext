Last summer, my brother Nick and cousin Jimmy traveled from England to visit us in Brooklyn — and this summer, they came again! We were overjoyed to have them. Here are a few photos from a wild week…

Toby was out of town with his dad, so Jimmy and Anton were fully in charge of the itinerary. They chose to see the Friends building, play soccer in Central Park, and eat their weight in Walkers crisps.

After a few days in New York, we decided to drive to my friend Sharon’s house in Connecticut. I was excited to see her, and the boys were excited to see her pool. Also, how GORGEOUS is her garden??? “I want to give my neighbors an experience,” she laughs. (Her full house tour is coming up soon.)

The boys swam and jumped on the trampoline…

…absolutely crushed this snack plate…

…and cooked up their usual antics. Boys this age always seem like a passel of puppies, tumbling all over the place.

One evening, Jimmy tried his first oyster, which he said “tasted like the ocean” and “wasn’t too bad.”

And Sharon, her husband, and I played Rummikub, which is such a fun game. Have you played?

Finally, we all headed home…

…and I got a quiet sofa all to myself to watch Love Is Blind. I was soooo fried but happy. Such a noisy, funny, wonderful week — we miss them already. xoxo

*(Joanna, in comments, on how the Connecticut visit worked)*

haha great question! we actually rented an Airbnb near our friends’ house in connecticut, since having four teenage boys sleeping over at their place seemed like way too much haha. but yes, with close friendships, I will often ask, “are you guys around this weekend? are you up for some visitors?” or something like that — and we’ve visited them in their town probably 20 times so they’re used to chaos landing on their doorstep haha.

with other friends, yes, as you say, it’s a mix of asking if we can come visit and having them tell us we have open invitations. and the same is true for our house, for sure. sharon and her husband have stayed with us many times, both when we’re in town and when we’re out of town (like, if they want a romantic nyc weekend together and crash at our house). and their kids have crashed at ours, too, even by themselves when they’re in the city for work or whatever. (I LOVE having them.) we have a pretty revolving door. the trick to this working well, I think, is the ability on both sides to say no. I’ve definitely said a certain weekend wouldn’t work out, and they’ve said the same, which is 100000% fine with zero subtext or hard feelings!
