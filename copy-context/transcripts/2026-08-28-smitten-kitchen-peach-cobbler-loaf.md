# Deb Perelman, peach cobbler loaf

A friend asked me last week how long it takes to get a recipe right. I told him the only answer I know: As long as it takes. I’d like to be someone who could “adjust my expectations,” make peace with “close enough,” or have the slightest amount of chill when it’s clear that whatever I’m trying to do isn’t something I can pull off, but we’re wired how we’re wired.

So, in the interest of full transparency, I’ve been trying to get this exact recipe right for the last six summers, and honestly, it’s a crime how many peaches have been surrendered to versions that came up short. Each stone fruit season, I tinkered and took copious notes — mostly variations on “this wasn’t it.” Diced peaches were somehow bland. Thinly-sliced ones disappeared entirely. Spices in the batter made the whole thing beige without making it any more flavorful. Eventually, I’d be at an impasse and fold my cards for the season.

It took forever because I wanted several things from it, and I couldn’t let any of them go:

I wanted a one-bowl cake, like my other favorite loaves. Ultimately, I accepted defeat on this, and this alone. (But you know I’m not over it.)

I wanted it to star big, chunky wedges of pie-like peaches — that is, spiced, fragrant, baked until tender and juicy — embedded and suspended throughout.

I wanted it to look like a peach crumble on top, with visible baked peaches lightly buckled into the cake beneath a mess of streusel.

And finally, because neat, rule-following baked goods are overrated — as are squat loaves that don’t fill out the pan — I liked it best when this cake looked a little chaotic. Teetering on, and occasionally slightly over, the edge feels a bit like my mental state on this 945th day of summer break (but who’s counting?). So if this cake looks a little messy, well, so am I.

I know we’re about two minutes from the end of peach season, but the cake doesn’t care. It’s ready, and I humbly think it’s worth the wait.
