If I have the time and energy, I really like to do a “power hour” of cooking when I unpack my vegetables so I have an easier time adding them to meals throughout the week. It also allows everything to take up less space in my fridge. I did this with everything except the kale and romano beans, both of which I just threw in the fridge to deal with enjoy later.

The first thing I did when I got home with my vegetables was tear the greens off of the beets and throw them in the compost, by which I mean my Mill (not sponsored! I just love that thing and think it was worth every penny even though it was so many pennies… here’s a referral code to get $150 off, which is just something anyone who owns a Mill can offer…not special!). Yes, beet greens are totally edible. But I don’t love them and I think it’s best to eat things that bring us joy. Moving on.

I threw the beetroots themselves, peel and all, into a saucepan and covered them with water. I brought the water to a boil and then covered the pot, turned the heat to low, and let the beets simmer until I could easily pierce them with a paring knife. I didn’t look at a clock. I just tried not to forget about them. I bet they were in there for about 45 minutes. Once they were tender, I rinsed them with cool water and then ripped a paper towel in half and put a half in each of my hands and rubbed the skin off the beets with the paper towels. Easy! I cut them into wedges, put them in a container, drizzled them with olive oil and sherry vinegar and seasoned them with salt and pepper. Once they were totally cool, I popped a lid on the container and parked them in the fridge to enjoy throughout the week. Marinated beets are great as part of a plate of lots of salads and things, as a topper for a rice bowl, as the base for a salad with other things like goat cheese…whatever!

Then I decided to make a sorta salsa/pesto type of mixture with the scallions and cilantro. So I trimmed off the root ends of the scallions, then cut them into inch-long pieces and put them on a parchment liner that fits in my air fryer. I tossed the chopped scallions with a little bit of olive oil and salt (using the parchment as a “bowl” so I didn’t have to wash an actual bowl) and then air fried them for about 6 minutes at 400ºF. Just long enough to get them a tiny bit charred and wilted. Then I put them in my blender with the entire bunch of cilantro, the juice of 2 limes and a big squeeze of olive oil (approximately 3 tablespoons). I seasoned this mixture with salt and put it in a jar. I’ve been eating it on everything. Tortilla chips. Scrambled eggs. Cold roast chicken. Anything!

Then I decided to turn on my actual oven, which I generally avoid in the summer, but it was early in the morning and pretty cool. So I cranked it to 425ºF. I cubed all four of my eggplants and put them on a sheet pan and drizzled them with olive oil and seasoned them with salt.

I did the same thing with all of my tomatoes. I debated keeping them fresh instead of cooked, but I was craving the concentrated flavor of roasted tomatoes so I went ahead and cooked them. I have access to these amazing tomatoes regularly so cooking a bunch of them at their peak is definitely a flex of my tomato privilege!
