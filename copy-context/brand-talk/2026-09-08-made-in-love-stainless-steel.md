# The stainless pan that sat unused for a year

He already cooks. Stainless still feels like too much: hard to tell a mistake from a good sear, hours scrubbing a rice pot, chicken thighs that leave brown bits he reads as a ruined pan. He dumps the flavor down the drain. He retreats to nonstick and cast iron — low risk, lower reward. The stainless frying pan sits unused for the better part of a year.

Someone asks why he never uses it. He brings it out for mushrooms. The pan softens them, then fills with their own juices. His heart sinks. Then the mushrooms shrink and pull that liquid back in. Styrofoam-bin mushrooms taste like something. He runs the same night the next day. Same result.

What he learns afterward is under the night: wine or stock lifts the brown bits into a pan sauce; a cleaning powder ends the scrub panic; the pan can sear hard then drop heat. The object earns the burner because the food changes and the scare ends — not because a feature list says so.
