# From novice to artist: she finally steams again

Liz already knows pour-over and espresso. Latte art is the one skill she has ducked. In high school, milk overflowed onto her hand while she looked away from the pitcher. The scar is still there fifteen years later. She treats steaming as the scary step, not a cute party trick.

She sets Eddy and Monty against the ordinary mugs in her cupboard. Coworkers and a couple of YouTube videos give her the map: fresh crema, silky foam, spout on the lip, tilt then lift. The map is not the story. The first pours fail. She leaves the foam in the pitcher. She holds the spout too far from the rim. She waits too long and the milk separates.

What happens when she keeps going: Eddy makes the whirlpool easy enough that she feels safe from another overflow. Every steam run comes out shiny and spoon-coating. Monty's shape lifts foam when a standard mug sinks it. The hearts are still clumsy after a pound of coffee and half a gallon of milk. She is not a master. She can see the night improving. The objects earn their place by making the avoided step practiceable again.
