And then, as the popping began, there he was again, moving the pan around like a man who had been handed something alive. Back and forth. Side to side. Quick wrists. Steady hands. The motion looked frantic, but it was not panic. It was practice.

Before we knew it, he was pouring popcorn into a bowl. It tasted like home. I did not know then that a person could taste safety.

Do you remember Jiffy Pop? It was basically an aluminum pie tin filled with gelatinous vegetable fat and popcorn kernels, covered by an aluminum foil top. The foil was coiled, so when the popcorn popped it rose into a dome. It looked like a miracle you could hold. He cut it open. I took a bite. It was terrible. Greasy. Flat. Disappointing in a way that felt personal, as if the box had promised me something and then laughed.

But before I could even say much, the pot was out. The oil was in. The kernels followed. Within ten minutes he repaired everything. The real popcorn returned, warm and forgiving. Then I climbed the stairs and sat in front of the TV with my Dad. Popcorn between us. Soda in hand. The world made right again.
