# Specific facts beat general praise.

**Source:** Public summaries of *Ogilvy on Advertising* (1983); Conversion.studio "Ogilvy on Advertising: Rules for Ads That Sell" (https://conversion.studio/blog/ogilvy-on-advertising, fetched 2026-08-31); Rob Palmer, same Ogilvy-on-AI recap (fetched 2026-08-31).
**Date added:** 2026-08-31

**Principle:**
A general claim ("quiet," "quality," "luxury") asks the reader to trust you. A specific claim ("the loudest noise is the clock") asks her to check you. Numbers, materials, named objects, named occasions. Superlatives without a fact get discounted.

**Popsmith failure mode:**
"Premium popcorn experience" / "heirloom quality" with no steel, no kits, no counter, no Friday. The brand already has specifics: multi-clad stainless, works on induction, kits in under 5 minutes, Oprah's Favorite Things (2023) as a named badge, not a vibe.

**One line we might run:**
Multi-clad stainless. On the stove in three minutes.

**Where this fails on Popsmith:**
Fact-dumping the patented spinner in line one. Specifics are proof under a territory, not the ad. Do not invent a number of layers, a review count, or a ROAS.
