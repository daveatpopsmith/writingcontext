# The ad must contain an offer.

**Source:** Public summaries of *Scientific Advertising* (1923): coupons, samples, a reason to reply; Hopkins tracked keyed offers, not applause (https://www.scientificadvertising.com/authors/hopkins/ and Pollins recap, fetched 2026-08-31).
**Date added:** 2026-08-31

**Principle:**
An advertisement is a salesperson. It asks for a next step that is small enough to take: sample, kit, shop the named thing, get the bundle. "Learn more" is not an offer. Neither is a brand manifesto with no door.

**Popsmith failure mode:**
Beautiful object, no next action. Or a fake offer ("unlock your best movie night"). Or stacking sale-aesthetic % off against a premium brand (macro-messaging: the offer should feel like generosity, not clearance).

**One line we might run:**
Shop the Popper.

**Where this fails on Popsmith:**
Turning every ad into a coupon. Most-aware traffic can take a hard offer. Cold traffic still needs a territory and a reason. Roma sets which. Peggy does not invent a discount.
