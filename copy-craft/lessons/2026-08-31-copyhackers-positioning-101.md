# Positioning is a sentence only we can say.

**Source:** Roxana Gramada, Copyhackers, "Positioning 101: How to live rent-free in your best clients' minds" (https://copyhackers.com/2026/04/positioning-101/). Dated April 2026 in the URL. Newest dated free post found from https://copyhackers.com/blog/ on 2026-08-31 (later 2026 months returned no posts). Full article loaded; not paywalled. Fetched 2026-08-31.
**Date added:** 2026-08-31

**Principle:**
Positioning is one sentence a competitor cannot steal: who it is for, what category it sits in, and the value the alternatives do not deliver. Write a plain descriptor first (this thing, for these people). Then find the memorable line. Keep the worksheet internal. The ad is the killer sentence, not the five interview questions that produced it.

**Popsmith failure mode:**
Pasting the worksheet into the ad: "For women who own Le Creuset, Popsmith is the premium stovetop popper that..." That dumps targeting language into customer-facing copy. Forbidden: women 40-70, persona names. The hunt for a "killer" also tempts banned overclaim (world's most advanced popcorn technology). Copyhackers is positioning freelance copywriters. Their ICP process is not our offer. Microwave-killer is a doorway, not the position.

**One line we might run:**
It earns the burner next to the Dutch oven.

**Where this fails on Popsmith:**
We already have a working killer in the roster. Do not reposition this week around the spinner, around Kernel-as-Poppy, or around a new category we invent. Mechanism is proof. Display and standard are the position. Dove's dry-skin bar was a daily necessity. The Popper is a ritual object that stays out. Do not force a necessity frame.
