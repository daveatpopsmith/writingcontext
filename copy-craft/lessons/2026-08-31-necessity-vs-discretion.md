# A pan proving durability is not proof anyone wants a popper.

**Source:** Observation, fetched 2026-08-31, from the same public DTC kitchen sources as `2026-08-31-dtc-kitchen-how-they-talk.md`, plus Popsmith brand guide: the competitive conversation is "us vs. the decision to settle," not us vs. All-Clad. Necessity goods argue "you will use this every night." Discretionary goods argue "this night becomes a night."
**Date added:** 2026-08-31

**Principle:**
Cookware ads can win on warping, PFAS, replacing a drawer of lids, surviving a 12-hour shift. That is necessity logic: she already cooks; she needs a better pan. A popper is discretionary. Durability, steel, dishwasher-safe, patented spinner — those answer "will it last?" They do not answer "why popcorn, why this, why now?" Desire first (territory). Proof second (steel, kits, reviews). Offer last.

**Popsmith failure mode:**
Leading with multi-clad as if she were shopping a skillet. "Built to last decades" with no Friday, no gift, no counter, no grandchild. Treating microwave-bag hate as if it were a warped-pan emergency. It is a preference, not a flood.

**One line we might run:**
This has become our Friday night tradition.

**Where this fails on Popsmith:**
Never showing craft. Discretion still needs a reason-why once she wants it. Do not swing to pure mood. Sequence: want → object → proof → next action.
