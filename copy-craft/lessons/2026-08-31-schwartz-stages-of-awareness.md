# Do not pitch the secret to the wrong stage.

**Source:** Public summaries of Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966): five stages (unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → product-aware → most-aware). Recaps: Mind Treks (https://mind-treks.com/breakthrough-advertising-summary-guide/, fetched 2026-08-31); Hawky (https://hawky.ai/blog/customer-awareness-stages, fetched 2026-08-31). Advertising channels desire. It does not create it. The headline must match what she already knows.
**Date added:** 2026-08-31

**Principle:**
A problem-aware reader ("microwave bags taste like the bag") does not need a secret origin story. She needs the solution named, then this product. A most-aware reader needs the offer, the color, the gift wrap. A fully unaware reader cannot be converted by a spec of the spinner.

**Popsmith failure mode:**
Cold traffic getting "Shop the bundle" with no want. Warm traffic getting a 45-second "what is stovetop popcorn" lecture. Pitching "the secret to movie night" to someone who already owns a Whirley-Pop.

**One line we might run:**
I didn't realize how much I missed stovetop popcorn until I made my first batch.

**Where this fails on Popsmith:**
Peggy does not diagnose the funnel from AdNova. Roma's concept implies the stage. If the concept is Homecoming, start in memory, not in SKU. If the concept is a sale to buyers, skip the memoir.
