# The headline does the work.

**Source:** Public recap of Ogilvy's "How to Create Advertising That Sells" (Gundir, https://gundir.com/resource/how-to-create-advertising-that-sells-by-david-ogilvy/, fetched 2026-08-31): five times as many people read the headline as the body; if the headline does not sell, most of the money is gone. Put the brand and the promise in it.
**Date added:** 2026-08-31

**Principle:**
Most people never reach the second sentence. The first line must name who it is for, what is on offer, or a fact worth stopping for. Clever-but-blank headlines waste the scroll. Include the proper noun when the reader would otherwise not know who ran the ad.

**Popsmith failure mode:**
"Experience the difference." "A better Friday." "Ready for real popcorn?" None of those need us. The brand-guide Do column already has working headlines: "The Le Creuset of popcorn makers." "Friday night, sorted."

**One line we might run:**
The Le Creuset of popcorn makers.

**Where this fails on Popsmith:**
Cramming Popsmith + benefit + joke + offer into one cramped static. The headline selects. The primary text sells. The CTA is the next action. Three jobs, three slots.
