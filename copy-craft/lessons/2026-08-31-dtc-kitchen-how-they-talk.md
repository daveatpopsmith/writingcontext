# How good DTC kitchen ads actually talk.

**Source:** Following-style observation from public web, fetched 2026-08-31. Not invented ROAS. Our Place copy notes (https://betweenthelinescopy.com/blog/our-place-case-study/; Tonelab voice, https://www.tonelab.so/brand/our-place). Made In (https://www.tonelab.so/brand/made-in; OptiMonk chef-led breakdown, https://www.optimonk.com/madein-cookware-marketing-breakdown). Great Jones (https://www.tonelab.so/brand/great-jones). Caraway "kitchen as character" reporting on D2C Times (public 2024–2026 recaps).
**Date added:** 2026-08-31

**Principle:**
The good ones do not open with a category lecture. They talk like a person in a kitchen:
- Named objects and jobs (Always Pan *replaces eight pieces*; Made In *same spec as the restaurant*; Great Jones *Cook like you mean it*).
- Command or present-tense statements, not mission statements.
- Beauty is shown; function is a short proof; gathering is the why for Our Place, skill is the why for Made In, nerve is the why for Great Jones.
- UGC and chef-education lead with a cooking moment, then the pan. Product is a prop that earns the close.

**Popsmith failure mode:**
Pasting "replaces 8 pans" onto a unitasker. Pasting chef-cred onto a popper no line cook owns. Pasting Caraway's three-act kitchen film onto a 15-second static. Their categories are daily-necessity cookware. Ours is a discrete, display-worthy ritual tool.

**One line we might run:**
Le Creuset for the braise. KitchenAid for the dough. Popsmith for the popcorn.

**Where this fails on Popsmith:**
Copying their community-and-values founder talk. Brand-guide already has the roster. Stay in Joanna / Padma / Kristen, not in Shiza Shahid or a chef network we do not have.
