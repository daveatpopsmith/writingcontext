# Put the brand in as a proper noun.

**Source:** Ogilvy "How to Create Advertising That Sells," public recap (Gundir, fetched 2026-08-31): most of his agency's headlines included the brand name and the promise, because skippers never meet the logo. Conversion.studio recap of the same rule (fetched 2026-08-31).
**Date added:** 2026-08-31

**Principle:**
Treat the name like a person's name. Not "our popper," not "this innovative maker," not "a stovetop system." If the headline could fit a competitor with one noun swap, the proper noun is missing. The name is a memory hook, not a watermark.

**Popsmith failure mode:**
"The Popper" with no Popsmith on a cold static. Calling it "Poppy." Calling the mascot Poppy. "Kernel" used as if it were the product. Generic "premium popper" that Google or Whirley-Pop could wear.

**One line we might run:**
Of course I have a Popsmith.

**Where this fails on Popsmith:**
Stamping POPSMITH in all caps on every frame until it looks like a clearance rack. The proper noun belongs in the line a skipper would still see. Not in a bug that fights the product shot.
