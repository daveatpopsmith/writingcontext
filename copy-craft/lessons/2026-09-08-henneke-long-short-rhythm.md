# Long takes her somewhere. Short tells the truth.

**Source:** Henneke Duistermaat, Enchanting Marketing, "Long vs Short Sentences: How to Take Your Readers on a Journey", https://www.enchantingmarketing.com/short-vs-long-sentences/, fetched 2026-09-08. Public craft post. Steal the mix. Do not paste her fiction examples. Do not buy the paid course from this drill.
**Date added:** 2026-09-08

**Principle:**
Rhythm is the mix. Long sentences take her on a journey (weather, hands, what happened). Short ones tell the gospel truth and make her pause. Three short punches in a row is a drum machine, not a person talking across a kitchen island. Read aloud. If every sentence is the same length, it is a bot. Roy Peter Clark via Henneke: long = journey, short = truth. Apple still varies them in sales copy. So can we.

**Popsmith failure mode:**
Grokbot fragments stacked as lander copy. "Every stove." "Stainless. Out." "Not the bag." Three punches, no journey, no warmth. Crisp used as staccato only. SOP-complete headlines that still sound like a product sheet because nothing breathes.

**One line we might run:**
I leave it out with the rest of the kitchen. It earns the counter.

**Where this fails on Popsmith:**
Do not write lyrical rain scenes about a popcorn maker. Do not open every section with a three-word punch just to prove you read Henneke. Do not turn Crisp into choppy dare copy. The short line still has to be a finished thought she would say, not a label.
