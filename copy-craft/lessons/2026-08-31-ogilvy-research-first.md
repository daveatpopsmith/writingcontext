# Research first. The line is found, not invented.

**Source:** Public summaries of *Ogilvy on Advertising* (1983) and *Confessions of an Advertising Man*; Rob Palmer, "David Ogilvy on AI" (https://robpalmer.com/blog/david-ogilvy-ai-copywriting, fetched 2026-08-31); Enchanting Marketing Rolls-Royce notes (https://www.enchantingmarketing.com/david-ogilvy-rolls-royce-advert/, fetched 2026-08-31).
**Date added:** 2026-08-31

**Principle:**
Do not start with a clever line. Start with the product, the reader, and the proof already in the file. Ogilvy spent weeks on Rolls-Royce before writing. The electric-clock headline was a fact sitting in a motor magazine, not a brainstorm. Research is the job. Wit is optional.

**Popsmith failure mode:**
Opening a Meta primary text with a vibe ("movie night magic") before opening `voice-of-customer.md` or the brand-guide Do/Don't table. Inventing a mechanism story instead of using the multi-clad steel, the kits, the handle.

**One line we might run:**
It's so beautiful I leave it on the counter all the time.

**Where this fails on Popsmith:**
Roma already handed a concept. Peggy's research is the context pack and the customer's words, not a new strategy deck. Do not "research" AdNova. Do not write a new origin myth for the spinner.
