# Case note: 1984 sold a worldview in one airing. (Craft only.)

**Source:** Craft notes, not a script reprint. Wikipedia "1984 (advertisement)" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_(advertisement), fetched 2026-08-31); Forbes interview with Steve Hayden (https://www.forbes.com/sites/allenstjohn/2014/01/31/12-lessons-in-creativity-from-the-greatest-super-bowl-ad-ever/, fetched 2026-08-31). Chiat/Day (Hayden, Clow, Thomas), Ridley Scott, Super Bowl XVIII. Almost no product. The heroine, not the spec sheet.
**Date added:** 2026-08-31

**Principle:**
The move: when you are launching against a giant, do not list ports. Stage a world, pick a villain (conformity, not a named rival in the cut), and let one image finish the sentence. Event advertising. One airing was the point.

**Popsmith failure mode:**
Dystopia, hammers, "think different" pastiche on a popcorn maker. Or a 60-second cinematic with no Popper and no next action. We are not launching a computer against IBM. We are a DTC object on Meta.

**One line we might run:**
(None from 1984. Do not write one in that register.)

**Where this fails on Popsmith:**
A kitchen appliance brand cannot copy event-cinema as a prospecting system. Steal only this: the product can stay almost offstage *if* the concept is already famous and the payoff names the thing. Ours is not. Show the Popper.
