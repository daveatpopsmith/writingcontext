# Don't ask in the first breath.

**Source:** Harry Dry, Marketing Examples, "Lizzy's email worked on me" (https://marketingexamples.com/sales/lizzy). Newest public example listed first on https://marketingexamples.com/newsletter and the homepage. Newsletter URL is a gallery, not a signup wall. Fetched 2026-08-31.
**Date added:** 2026-08-31

**Principle:**
The message that got a yes was the one that did not ask for one. Harry answered a single cold email out of 97 because it put no meeting on him. Lizzy's move was to stop requesting a quick call and to say she was not asking for anything. People replied. The sale can still happen (Harry signed up). The first line's job is to take the burden off the reader, not to close.

**Popsmith failure mode:**
Running outreach manners as an ad: "I'm not asking you to buy." That is inbox voice, not kitchen-island voice. Also: do not import Lizzy's reply-rate jump as a Popsmith proof point. Other brands teach craft. They do not set our offer. No invented reviews.

**One line we might run:**
It sits with the Le Creuset. Nobody had to be sold.

**Where this fails on Popsmith:**
The lander still needs Shop the Popper. No-ask is a first-line tactic, not a page with the CTA stripped. Warm and retargeting can ask. Cold can recognize. Do not stall with "Ready to upgrade movie night?" then Shop Now. Do not lead with the spinner to manufacture urgency the first line just refused.
