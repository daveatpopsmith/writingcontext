# Case note: The clock was a researched proof of quiet.

**Source:** Craft notes, not a reprint of the 1958 body copy. Enchanting Marketing (https://www.enchantingmarketing.com/david-ogilvy-rolls-royce-advert/, fetched 2026-08-31); Lewis Folkard breakdown (https://lewisfolkard.co.uk/ad-breakdown-rolls-royces-electric-clock-david-ogilvy/, fetched 2026-08-31). Headline attributed to a Motor magazine technical editor; Ogilvy found it in research.
**Date added:** 2026-08-31

**Principle:**
The move: do not say "quiet luxury." Show one odd, checkable detail that can only be true if the whole machine is right. Long headline. Brand in the line. Body is a stack of facts, not mood.

**Popsmith failure mode:**
Hunting for a fake "clock" ("the loudest sound is the kernel" as invented poetry). If a fact is not in product knowledge or a review, it is not a clock. Our real clocks: the handle, the counter, the kits, the steel.

**One line we might run:**
The handle turns. The rest takes care of itself.

**Where this fails on Popsmith:**
A kitchen appliance is not a Rolls. Silence is not the desire. Do not write automotive-luxury pastiche. Steal the method (research → one proof), not the register.
