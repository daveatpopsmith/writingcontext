I had a Whirley Pop that made okay popcorn, which would have been fine if getting it out weren't such a mess. I had to shake it loose every time, so I used it once or twice and forgot it in the back of the cabinet. Another ugly, cumbersome kitchen gadget.

I almost didn't try The Popsmith Popper, because I already owned something that popped corn. Then I made a batch on the same burner as the rest of my cookware.

### I stood at the sink with it.

Photo: Dave 2026-09-01. File `whirley-lander-assets/popcorn-vibes-in-the-new-kitchen.png`. Four-panel: Whirley Pop on the stove (disgust) vs Butter Popsmith Popper with gold handle (approval). Overlay: POPCORN VIBES IN THE NEW KITCHEN. This slot only. Face is in the meme on purpose.

It's aluminum. You can't put a Whirley Pop in the dishwasher, because the cycle will discolor it, strip the metal, leave a film, and corrode it. I wiped it with a paper towel, or I stood at the sink with this greasy, lightweight pan. I did both.

The Popsmith Popper goes in with the rest of the dishes. Lid, body, stirring arm. I don't wash it by hand anymore.

### The lid is two clips.

The gears are nylon. When the bowl fills, the crank gets heavy and the teeth skip. I've had those clips let go and dump popcorn on the burner. You can buy another lid when that happens. It's cheaply designed.

### I hid it because I didn't want anyone to see it.

It didn't belong in a modern kitchen, and it was awful to look at.

### Popsmith is the best popcorn I've ever made at home.

I put kernels and oil in The Popsmith Popper and turned the handle over medium heat. About five minutes later I had a full bowl, still hot, and people were already coming into the kitchen. Someone always wants the handle. This is the popcorn I want to serve.

### It is beautiful in my kitchen.

I got the gold handles to match the fixtures, and cream because it sits against the backsplash. People comment on it. I use it all the time. I'd miss it if I put it away. It lives on the stove.
