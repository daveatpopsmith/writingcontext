# Dad rituals research — Popsmith angle (2026-09-15)

For C9 yapper / Fridays She'll Remember. Peggy dig (not Tarly — his lane is newsletters).

## The struggle (what dads say / research says)

1. **Ideas are the wall.** HCI lockdown research (ACM, UK fathers): dads want shared *non-digital* activities but struggle with idea generation — “what do we actually do?” — especially when usual outings are off the table. ([Fathers, Young Children and Technology](https://doi.org/10.1145/3555188))

2. **Screens win the competition.** Journal of Fatherhood & Early Childhood Development interviews: dads feel they “can’t compete” with Fortnite / phones (“play Fortnite or hang out with dad?”). Technoference cuts conversation both ways — kid screens and dad’s phone. ([JFDE 2022](https://doi.org/10.53956/jfde.2022.151); [Technoference PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5681450/))

3. **Work eats the calendar.** Fathers Network Scotland *Time With Dad*: practical barriers dominate — work timing, shortage of father-friendly activities, half of dads rarely feel connected to other local parents. ([Report](https://www.fathersnetwork.org.uk/time_with_dad_report))

4. **Ritual > schedule.** Dad blogs and guides converge: one short, predictable, non-negotiable ritual beats a packed weekend. 10–15 minutes of undivided attention can be the anchor. Kids need to know *that* moment will happen. ([Daddicted non-negotiable rituals](https://www.daddicted.com/en/guidance/non-negotiable-rituals-the-ultimate-hack-to-be-a-present-dad-without-blowing-up-your-schedule/); [Dad Day family culture](https://www.dadday.co/how-to-build-family-culture-dads-guide/); [Dim Dads rituals](https://dimdads.com/create-family-rituals-together/))

5. **“Action talk” is how many dads open up.** Fatherhood Project *Ritual Dad Time*: men often talk while doing something (shooting baskets, cooking, cranking) — Pollack’s “action talk.” Face-to-face dinner can fail where side-by-side at the stove works. ([PDF](https://www.thefatherhoodproject.org/media/Create-Ritual-Dad-Time.pdf))

6. **Moms notice the gap.** 100 Days of Real Food: “Coming up with ways to hang out with your kids… isn’t always easy” — especially when dad doesn’t share the kid’s usual interests (shopping, nail salon, “girly” movie). Cooking date / kitchen handoff is pitched as the workaround. ([Daddy Daughter Cooking Date Night](https://www.100daysofrealfood.com/daddy-daughter-cooking-night/))

**One-line territory for C9:** Popsmith is the shortest path to a weekly Dad ritual that kids participate in, that doesn’t need a plan, and that creates the talk — then the movie.

**Do not:** nostalgia / “remember when”; chore language for the kid; guests/game-night second door; price as the hook.

**Do:** Dad as hero of a night she can count on; hands on the crank; smells like a theater; Fridays she'll remember.

## Father–daughter / Dad’s voice (2026-09-15 pass 2)

Not “Mom’s things” as objects. Mom is the center of a kid’s universe. Dad is looking for a lane — his time to shine, his voice with her.

### The relationship gap
- **All Pro Dad:** “many dads struggle to find ways to connect with their daughters and stay close.” Unique ability, but they don’t know the how. ([4 Ways](https://allprodad.com/4-ways-to-have-a-good-father-daughter-relationship/))
- **r/daddit (girl dads):** “drawn more to my SO than me… She always wants my SO and never me. The only time she comes to me is to ask me to buy something.” Tried connecting through *his* hobbies (RC, drones, games); she’s not into that. ([thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/daddit/comments/1hs3k61/girl_dads_how_do_you_connect_with_your_daughters/))
- **National Center for Fathering:** dads leave the heavy/vulnerable talk to Mom. If Dad doesn’t initiate, “every other voice will speak into her life except yours.” ([5 Power Strategies](https://fathers.com/articles/5-power-strategies-a-dad-can-use-to-lead-his-daughter))
- **Dad of Divas / Cheryl Rosenberg:** dads try to *fix*; daughters need presence and a short weekly check-in (“I want to hear what you’ve done this week”). Connection doesn’t come from fixing. ([Healing Artfully](https://www.dadofdivas.com/podcast/healing-artfully-creative-ways-for-dads-to-connect-with-their-daughters))
- **Dad & Daughter Connection podcast:** one-on-one is the magic — “When it’s just the two of us, you can connect on a deeper level.” Park/ice cream fade as they grow; “little weird normal moments” replace them. ([Apple](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/the-dad-daughter-connection/id1795444764))

### Why Mom is the center (research, not a slam)
- Attachment literature long treated fathers as “the other parent.” Bonding is still coded as mother–child first. ([Boba / paternal bonding review](https://eu.boba.com/pages/the-importance-of-dads); [PMC paternal bonding](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9690989/))
- New-dad qualitative work: mother–infant bond can leave Dad “on the sideline,” even jealousy that’s shameful to admit. Fathers then hunt for *their own* bonding activity (bath, hold, feed, play) — a parallel closeness, not a replacement. ([NOP2 fatherhood emotions](https://doi.org/10.1002/nop2.1482); breastfeeding father studies)

### What actually works (maps to Popsmith)
- Side-by-side / action talk, not a sit-down interrogation.
- A named, repeating one-on-one she can count on (daddy-daughter breakfast, Friday night).
- He joins *her* world for a few minutes; she doesn’t have to join RC cars.
- Presence, not a lecture. The crank is the check-in.

**Copy bar:** Mom is her universe. Friday Popsmith is Dad’s night to shine — the few minutes she actually talks to *him*.
