# Onions that scatter, so two boards split the jobs

**Source:** Material Kitchen journal, "Why you want both a wood and plastic cutting board", https://materialkitchen.com/blogs/design/why-you-want-both-a-wood-and-plastic-cutting-board, brand journal, published date not on page, fetched 2026-09-01.
**Who is talking:** The brand as a cook. Chopped onions that never make it to the pan. A complicated relationship with plastic. A year looking for a board that can go in the dishwasher without adding virgin plastic.
**The walk:** Wood for the chicken and the roast, oil so it takes a patina. Plastic because they wanted the dishwasher and not another virgin board. Angled edges because the mince used to hit the counter. A towel under the board so it does not skate. Two sizes because snack and roast are different jobs.
**One steal:** Same as the spoons card. Two materials split the jobs she already has. Start from the onion on the counter.
**One never-steal:** FSC sermon as the case, chef-feet as talent chrome, "workhorses that moonlight as serving pieces," last-a-lifetime, antimicrobial scare.
**Collector:** almost. Identical to the already-yes spoons pattern (object-life, two materials, jobs she has). Still almost for Roma. Do not mark yes.
