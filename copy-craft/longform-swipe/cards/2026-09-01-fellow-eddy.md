# Easy object, two-year crease that kept splitting

**Source:** Fellow journal, "Perfecting Steamed Milk: The Making of Eddy Steaming Pitcher", https://fellowproducts.com/blogs/learn/perfecting-steamed-milk-the-making-of-eddy-steaming-pitcher, Hanna McPhee (product manager), published date not on page, fetched 2026-09-01.
**Who is talking:** PM who sat in cafés and kept a folder of baristas' hands on pitchers. They thought a pitcher with no electronics would be simple.
**The walk:** Easy, right — then two years. Baristas hate the pelican gullet (two flow rates). They crease the face so milk moves at one speed. Metal that is cylindrical does not want a sharp crease and splits. Handle 180: they stop chasing the perfect grip and make a ribbon you can hold like a pencil.
**One steal:** The manufacturing refusal (the crease splitting) is more convincing than a feature named "intuitive pour."
**One never-steal:** Barista-champ talent as the buyer, "end all be all handle," shop-the-collection close.
**Collector:** almost. Home espresso person who already owns a pitcher and can feel the gullet. Not chef-as-hero if we steal the loop, not the café costume.
**Territory fit:** Craftsman's Choice.
