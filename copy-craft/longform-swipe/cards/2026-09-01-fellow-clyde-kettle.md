# Take the tired kettle apart, then put back only what pours

**Source:** Fellow journal, "Redesigning The Classic Whistling Tea Kettle", https://fellowproducts.com/blogs/learn/redesigning-the-classic-whistling-tea-kettle, Julian Bagirov (designer) and Jake Miller, published date not on page, fetched 2026-09-01.
**Who is talking:** Designer and founder. Object-life: the clunky whistling kettle that lives on every American stove.
**The walk:** They throw out the classic and start at the handle (weight in the hand). The scream-whistle is the pain, so they spend months on reeds that sound like a note, and drop the hot spout lid so you pour with one hand. Because 1.7 L is heavy, so the handle has to feel luxurious and not cheap.
**One steal:** Process as proof. Name the object everyone already owns, then one physical fix at a time.
**One never-steal:** Grandma's house as the claim (they mention it as the tired fixture, not as homecoming). Feature list of a third kettle. "Add Clyde to your collection" shop close.
**Collector:** almost. Object-literate kitchen. Coffee/tea gear she would already have opinions about.
**Territory fit:** Craftsman's Choice + Timeless Object as replacement for the clunky stove kettle.
