# Makers' walk: eight days of clay, then the lid that fits the pan she owns

**Source:** Our Place journal (Your Place), "All About the Tagine | How Our Traditionware Is Made", https://fromourplace.com/blogs/your-place/all-about-the-tagine, Steph Hong (Sr. Product Development Manager), published 2023-01-13, fetched 2026-09-01.
**Who is talking:** The product lead, not a talent cook. She is walking how the lid is made and why it has to sit on the Always Pan they already sell.
**The walk:** Hand-poured mold, knob thrown on, lid measured on their pan so steam stays. Eight to nine days: bisque, glaze, paint, fire. Conical lid is the self-baste. Morocco is the workshop, not a mood. Consultant cooks wanted runny vs thick, so they add a steam slot. Then a recipe guest appears — don't steal that half.
**One steal:** Process as proof, tied to an object the Collector already owns (the pan the lid must fit).
**One never-steal:** Chosen-family copy, Nasim-as-hero recipe block, lead/cadmium scare vs "some traditional tagines," dishwasher-safe spec pile.
**Collector:** almost. She might already own the Always Pan. This is why a lid exists, not a heritage pitch. Roma can flag.
**Territory fit:** Craftsman's Choice. Not Homecoming.
