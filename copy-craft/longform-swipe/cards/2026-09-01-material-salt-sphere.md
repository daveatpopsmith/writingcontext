# The well she wouldn't quit, then the opening that had to fit his hand

**Source:** Material Kitchen, "The Salt Sphere Design Journey with Eunice", https://materialkitchen.com/blogs/feature/behind-the-design-the-salt-sphere, Eunice Byun (interview), published date not on page (piece opens in early 2022), fetched 2026-09-01.
**Who is talking:** A designer who cooks at home. She already has a salt well she keeps using even as it falls apart. A teammate still uses her father's sugar bowl.
**The walk:** Objects people refuse to put away, then their own tired pieces. They live with a prototype. Her hand fits. His doesn't, so the opening changes. The test is whether they miss it when it goes back to the office. A metal handle gets cut because it made the sphere feel like a thing you already own from somewhere else.
**One steal:** Ask whether the house will miss it when the prototype leaves. If not, it doesn't belong.
**One never-steal:** Homecoming as the brief. Limited-drop sold-out close. Patina / gets-better-with-age. Design-inspiration costume.
**Collector:** almost. The kitchen already owns the object she respects. Object-life, not a pan try. Roma will pass. Never steal the Material Object drop chrome or the sentimental-items setup.
**Territory fit:** Timeless Object as replacement for the well that's breaking. Craftsman's Choice on the miss-it test.
