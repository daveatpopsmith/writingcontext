# Elevate the kitschy rest so she leaves it out

**Source:** Material Kitchen, "The Resting Stone Design Journey with Eunice", https://materialkitchen.com/blogs/feature/behind-the-design-the-resting-stone, Eunice Byun (interview), published 2023-10-11, fetched 2026-09-02.
**Who is talking:** A designer who cooks at home. She has wanted a utensil rest in the line since 2019 because the category is functional and kitschy, not special.
**The walk:** Skepticism is the category itself (silly rests that scream their job). Try starts in materials (glass for color and opacity) and the pebble form. A pop-up customer asks for several small ones she can park on both sides of the stove, not one bulky nest. Happened: the object stops reading as spoon-rest chrome. She leaves it out. Sold as a pair. One for utensils, one for a dish brush, one for jam on a board.
**One steal:** Customer friction ("I wish I could have more than one and they weren't so bulky") becomes the product logic (pairs; scatter across the kitchen). Dated/kitschy tool elevated into something she'd display. Same move as Whirley without saying last-one.
**One never-steal:** Material Object / heirloom / sentimental / vintage '60s–'70s permanence chrome. Bedroom/bathroom as proof it belongs everywhere. Limited-drop Manifesto.
**Collector:** yes. She already lives among chosen objects. This elevates a tool she'd leave out beside cookware she respects, not a beginner kit.
**Territory fit:** Craftsman's Choice / dated-tool elevation. Soft Whirley-adjacent: replace the silly rest, not the whole kitchen.
