## What a headline is

A headline is a complete statement that stops her because it says a thought. Not a label. Not a spec chip. Not a caption. Not a clever recap of the enemy. Caples: the headline's job is to get the next line read, by promising. Ogilvy: most people never reach the body. If this line does not sell, the reason does not exist.

## Tests (all must pass)

1. **Statement.** Subject and a claim. Not a noun phrase (`Stainless steel, made to sit out...`). Not a bullet with a comma (`It works on every stovetop, and cleanup is the dishwasher.`). "It works..." is already explaining. Headlines interrupt.
2. **Billboard.** Rip the body off. Would she pause. If the line only works as a caption under a photo, it is not a headline.
3. **It SAYS something.** A thought she can want, or a sharper version of one she already has. Information she could file in a spec table does not count as saying something. Clear is not the same as mute.
4. **Swap.** Put the line on a kettle, a pan, a blender. If it still fits, kill it. Hopkins: the flag has to be this product's.
5. **Cover the body.** Does this line earn the price by itself ($229 on this lander). If the paragraph has to finish the wanting, you promoted a proof line.
6. **Value, not worksheet.** Numbered reasons: the number is the value (what she gets in her kitchen). Stainless, induction, dishwasher, stirring arm, the bag: those are proof in the body. Copyhackers: keep the positioning worksheet internal. Run the line she can remember.
7. **Right stage.** Continue the ad. Do not recap it. After a Zone 3 microwave ad, do not headline the bag. Schwartz: do not pitch the enemy to someone who just watched the enemy lose. Never "you just watched the video."
8. **Discretionary sequence.** Desire first. Proof second. Offer last. Steel and dishwashers answer will it last. They do not answer why popcorn, why this, why now.
9. **Remarkable (Godin).** Would she mention this to a friend. Specs are not remarkable. Permission: a $200 line is an invitation, not a shout. Smallest viable: write for the woman who already leaves beautiful things out, not for snackers. Source: seths.blog, not the books.

## Voice gates (Dave)

- Never call The Popper a pot. People hear sauce pot or stockpot. Say The Popper, or stainless steel stovetop popcorn maker.
- Complete sentences. No Grokbot fragments (`Every stove.` `Stainless. Out.`). The verb stays in the line.
- Less clutter. Make the statement. Stop. Do not add clauses for show.
- $200 impulse: friendly, positive, persuasive. Not a dare. Not a shove.
- Kitchen island: if you would not say it to a woman who already owns Le Creuset, it does not ship.

## Pass shape (the job, not a template)

A friend would say the line. Then the body proves it with steel, time, stove, dishwasher.

She says the popcorn is actually good. She leaves it on the stove. Friday got easy. That is the copy. The specs keep her from talking herself out of $229. They are not why she talks herself into it.
